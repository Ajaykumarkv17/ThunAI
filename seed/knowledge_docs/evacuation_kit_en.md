# What to Take — Evacuation Kit Guidance

Preparing a go-bag before a flood warning arrives saves precious minutes when
you actually need to leave. This guide lists what a practical household
evacuation kit should contain and how to keep it ready.

## Keep it ready, not scattered

- Pack your kit into one or two bags that a single adult can carry, so the
  whole household is not searching separately at the last minute.
- Store the kit somewhere reachable even if the ground floor floods first —
  for example on a shelf above likely water height, not in a low cupboard.
- Check and refresh the kit every few months: replace expired medicines,
  charge power banks, and check torch batteries.

## Documents and money

- Original or photocopied identification documents (Aadhaar, ration card,
  voter ID, property papers) in a waterproof pouch or a sealed plastic bag.
- A small amount of cash. During flood events, card machines and ATMs may be
  offline due to power cuts or network outages, so cash on hand matters.
- A written list of important phone numbers (family, doctor, ward
  coordinator, insurance agent), in case your phone runs out of charge or
  loses signal.

## Health and medicine

- At least a few days' supply of any regular prescription medicine for every
  household member, plus a copy of the prescription if space allows.
- A basic first-aid kit: adhesive bandages, antiseptic, any medicine a family
  member needs regularly, oral rehydration salts, and a thermometer if you
  have one.
- Spare spectacles or contact lens supplies for anyone who needs them.

## Water, food, and light

- Drinking water in sealed bottles or containers, enough for at least one
  day per person if possible.
- Non-perishable, ready-to-eat food that needs no cooking: biscuits, dry
  snacks, energy bars, or similar.
- A torch with spare batteries, or a hand-crank/solar torch that does not
  depend on batteries running out during a prolonged power cut.
- A power bank, charged in advance, for keeping a mobile phone usable when
  mains power is unavailable for an extended period.

## Clothing and comfort

- One change of dry clothing per person, packed in a waterproof bag or a
  sealed plastic cover.
- A light blanket or shawl, particularly important for children, elderly
  household members, and anyone at a shelter overnight.
- Simple hygiene items: soap, a small towel, sanitary supplies, and any items
  specifically needed by infants (nappies, formula) or elderly members.

## For households with special needs

- If a household member uses a mobility aid, hearing aid, or other assistive
  device, keep spare batteries or a backup device with the kit where
  possible, and note the device's specific needs on the written phone-number
  list so a responder can help quickly if needed.
- For infants: extra feeding supplies, a light carrier or sling that frees
  the carer's hands while moving through water or crowds.
- For anyone with a chronic condition (for example diabetes or a heart
  condition), keep a written note of the condition and current medicines in
  the kit, in case a health worker needs this information at a shelter.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] ID documents in a waterproof pouch
- [ ] Cash for at least a day or two
- [ ] Written list of important phone numbers
- [ ] Several days of regular prescription medicine
- [ ] Basic first-aid kit and oral rehydration salts
- [ ] Sealed drinking water and ready-to-eat food
- [ ] Torch, spare batteries, and a charged power bank
- [ ] One change of dry clothing per person, packed waterproof
- [ ] Special items for infants, elderly, or household members with a
  disability or chronic condition
