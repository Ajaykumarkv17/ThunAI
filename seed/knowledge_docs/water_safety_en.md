# Flood Water Safety Basics

Flood water is almost never as safe as it looks. Even water that seems calm and
shallow can carry hidden dangers: open drains, sharp debris, electrical hazards,
and disease-causing organisms. This guide covers what to do before, during, and
after contact with flood water.

## Before you go near flood water

- Assume all flood water is contaminated. It can carry sewage, chemicals, and
  agricultural runoff even if it looks clear.
- Do not let children play in or near flood water, including water pooled in
  courtyards or on roads.
- If flood water is moving, even ankle-deep moving water can knock an adult off
  their feet. Six inches (about 15 cm) of fast-moving water is enough to sweep
  a person away.
- Never drive or ride a two-wheeler through flooded roads. A large share of
  flood deaths happen inside vehicles that stall in rising water. If your
  vehicle stalls in water, leave it and move to higher ground immediately.

## Electrical safety around flood water

- Treat every puddle near a building, pole, or transformer as potentially
  live. Flood water can conduct electricity from downed lines, submerged
  wiring, or damaged meters.
- If you see a downed power line anywhere near water, do not approach it or
  touch anything it is resting on. Keep others away and report the location
  to the local electricity board and to your ward coordinator.
- Do not switch on mains power in a flooded home until a qualified electrician
  has checked the wiring. Water-damaged wiring can cause fires or shocks well
  after the flood recedes.
- During a power cut, use a torch or battery lamp rather than candles near
  water-soaked areas, since spilled fuel or gas leaks are more likely after
  flooding.

## If you must enter flood water

- Wear closed footwear, never bare feet or sandals. Submerged glass, nails,
  and metal sheeting are common causes of injury.
- Avoid entering water with open wounds or cuts where possible. If you must,
  cover the wound with a waterproof dressing first and clean it thoroughly
  afterwards.
- Wash your hands, feet, and any exposed skin with soap and clean water as
  soon as you are able, and again after any further contact.
- Do not use flood water for drinking, cooking, or brushing teeth, even after
  boiling, unless you have no other option and boiling is your only
  purification method available. Boiling for at least one full minute kills
  most disease-causing organisms, but boiling does not remove chemical
  contamination.

## Drinking water and food

- Store safe drinking water in sealed containers before flood water rises, if
  you have advance warning.
- If your usual water source may be contaminated, use a mobile-carrier
  connection to check community updates, since local news of safe water
  distribution points is often shared over SMS or WhatsApp broadcast lists
  when landlines and piped water are disrupted.
- Discard any food that has come into contact with flood water, including
  food in containers that are not fully sealed and airtight.
- If drinking water quality is in doubt and you have no way to boil it, treat
  it with a water-purification tablet if available, following the package
  instructions exactly.

## Recognising illness after flood water exposure

Waterborne illness can appear days after exposure, not only immediately. Watch
for fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, skin rashes, or unusual fatigue in yourself,
children, and elderly family members.

**This document provides general safety information only and is not medical
advice.** If you or anyone in your household develops these symptoms, or has a
wound that becomes red, swollen, or painful, contact a doctor or your nearest
health centre. For a medical emergency, call [LOCAL EMERGENCY HELPLINE NUMBER]
or go to the nearest hospital.
