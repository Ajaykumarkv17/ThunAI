# After the Flood — Returning Home Safely

Once floodwater recedes and a coordinator or official notice says it is safe
to return, several risks remain inside and around the home. This guide covers
the first steps for a safe return.

## Before you go inside

- Wait for official confirmation from your ward coordinator or an equivalent
  authority that it is safe to return, rather than assuming safety just
  because the water outside has visibly dropped.
- Look for structural damage from the outside first: cracked walls, a
  visibly leaning structure, or a sagging roofline. If you see any of these
  signs, do not enter the building. This document does not provide a
  structural safety assessment — treat visible structural damage as a signal
  to wait for a qualified inspector, not as something to judge yourself.
- Check for downed power lines, damaged gas connections, or the smell of gas
  near the property before entering, and keep well away if you notice any of
  these.

## Inside the home

- Do not switch on mains electricity until a qualified electrician has
  inspected the wiring. Water-damaged electrical systems are a fire and
  shock risk even after they appear dry.
- Ventilate the house by opening doors and windows before spending extended
  time inside, especially if there is a smell of stagnant water, mould, or
  gas.
- Discard any food, medicine, or cosmetics that came into contact with flood
  water, including anything in containers that are not fully sealed.
- Photograph damage to your home and belongings before starting cleanup, if
  you plan to apply for any relief assistance or insurance claim, since this
  record is easy to lose once cleanup begins.

## Cleaning up

- Wear gloves and closed footwear while cleaning, since flood-soaked
  materials can hide sharp debris and contamination.
- Mould can begin developing within one to two days in wet conditions.
  Removing and drying wet materials (soaked bedding, carpets, cardboard)
  promptly reduces this risk.
- Wash your hands thoroughly after any cleanup work and before eating or
  touching your face.

## Health after returning

Returning to a flood-affected home can expose you to mould, contaminated
residue, and stress on top of physical exhaustion.

**This document provides general safety information only and is not medical
advice.** Watch for symptoms such as persistent cough, skin irritation, fever,
or unusual fatigue in yourself and family members during cleanup, and contact
a doctor or your nearest health centre if these appear. For a medical
emergency, call [LOCAL EMERGENCY HELPLINE NUMBER] or go to the nearest
hospital. If a structural concern is identified, contact a qualified engineer
or your local authority rather than attempting a repair yourself, since this
document does not provide structural repair guidance.

## Looking out for the community

- Check on neighbours as you return, especially elderly residents and anyone
  who evacuated alone, since they may need help with cleanup or may not yet
  know it is safe to return.
- Report any hazard you notice while returning — a downed line, a damaged
  road surface, a gas smell — to your ward coordinator so it can be recorded
  and addressed for the whole street, not just your own household.
