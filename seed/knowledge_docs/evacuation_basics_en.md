# Evacuation Basics for a Rising River

This guide covers the practical steps for leaving your home safely when a
river reach near your community is rising and an evacuation has been advised.

## Recognising when to leave

- Do not wait for water to enter your home before deciding to leave. Roads
  can flood and become impassable well before water reaches your doorstep.
- Pay attention to alerts from your ward coordinator, community messaging
  groups, or official channels. If an alert names your area or your street,
  treat it as directed at your household, not as general information.
- If mobile network coverage is patchy, do not assume "no alert reached me"
  means "no evacuation is needed." When in doubt, check with a neighbour or
  head toward the nearest known shelter.

## Before you leave the house

- Switch off the electricity mains and the gas supply if it is safe to reach
  them without wading through water. Do not risk touching switches or meters
  that are already wet or that you must reach by standing in water.
- Move any items you cannot carry — important documents you are not taking,
  electronics, furniture — to the highest point in the house that is
  reasonably reachable, such as a loft, upper shelf, or upper floor.
- Take your prepared evacuation kit (see the "What to Take" guidance) rather
  than trying to pack in the moment.
- Lock the house if it is safe and quick to do so, but do not delay
  evacuation for this if water is already rising around the property.

## Moving safely

- Walk, do not run, through any standing water, and use a stick or pole to
  check depth and footing ahead of you if the water is murky.
- Keep children within arm's reach at all times, and hold hands rather than
  relying on children to keep pace on their own.
- Avoid walking or driving across a bridge or causeway if water is flowing
  over it or close to the top of it — the road surface underneath may
  already be damaged or washed away even if it looks passable.
- If you see fast-flowing water, do not attempt to cross it on foot, on a
  two-wheeler, or in a car, even if it looks shallow. Wait for a responder or
  find an alternative route to higher ground.
- Move as a household group rather than splitting up, where possible, so
  that no one is unaccounted for once you reach the shelter.

## If you are trapped by rising water

- Move to the highest safe point available — an upper floor, a roof accessed
  safely from inside, or a raised structure — rather than attempting a risky
  crossing.
- Signal for help: a bright cloth, a torch light at night, or calling out are
  all ways rescuers can locate you. If you have mobile signal, contact the
  helpline or your ward coordinator and give your exact location, including
  any nearby landmark.
- Stay where rescuers can find you. Do not attempt to swim through moving
  flood water unless there is truly no other option and staying in place is
  clearly more dangerous.

## Helping others while evacuating

- Check on elderly neighbours living alone, and on any household you know has
  a member with a mobility difficulty, before assuming everyone nearby is
  already moving to safety.
- If someone needs help moving but you cannot safely assist them yourself
  (for example, moving a wheelchair through water), tell a responder or the
  coordinator their location as soon as you can, rather than delaying your
  own evacuation to attempt it alone.
- Do not leave a family member behind on the assumption that "someone else
  will check on them" — confirm it directly if you can.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Leave when advised; do not wait for water at your doorstep
- [ ] Switch off electricity and gas mains only if safe to reach
- [ ] Take your prepared evacuation kit
- [ ] Move as a household group, keeping children within arm's reach
- [ ] Never cross fast-flowing or rising water on foot or by vehicle
- [ ] If trapped, move to the highest safe point and signal for help
- [ ] Check on neighbours who may need help moving, and report their
  location to a responder if you cannot assist directly
